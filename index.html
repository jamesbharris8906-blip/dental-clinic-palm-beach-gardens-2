What a first visit looks like

A first visit usually starts with a talk about your health, your past dental work, and any pain or worry you carry into the room. Then come digital x-rays when needed, a check of teeth and gums, and a cleaning if the timing is right. The dentist will point out what looks healthy, what needs watching, and what should be fixed soon. You will hear the options in order of need, with clear notes on what each option does and what it does not do. If a tooth needs a filling, you will know why a filling is enough. If a tooth needs a crown, you will know why a filling would not hold.

People often ask how often to visit. For most adults, twice a year works well. That rhythm lets the team catch decay early, check gum health, remove tartar that a brush cannot reach, and keep records that show change over time. Children, people with gum disease, and people with dry mouth or a high risk of decay may need a different rhythm, and the team will say so plainly.

Cost is a fair question, and it deserves a straight answer before work begins. The office works with a range of insurance plans and offers CareCredit and Cherry financing. Ask for an estimate in writing, ask what insurance may cover, and ask what happens if a plan changes. A clear estimate removes the guesswork and lets you choose with facts in hand.

General Dentistry

General Dentistry covers the day-to-day care that keeps teeth and gums in good shape: exams, cleanings, digital x-rays, fillings, and gum checks. At Steven Weiss DDS, a routine visit is also a chance to talk about brushing, floss, diet, grinding at night, and habits that wear teeth down. Small changes at home often make the biggest difference between visits. A softer brush, a fluoride toothpaste, and a steady routine can slow decay and keep gums from bleeding.

Gum health deserves the same care as teeth. Bleeding when you floss, gums that pull back, or breath that stays stale can be signs that gums need care. Early gum care is far easier than late care, and it protects the bone that holds teeth in place. If deep cleaning is needed, the team will explain each step, how the mouth may feel after, and how to keep the result.

Fillings have changed a lot over the years. Tooth-coloured materials can match the shade of nearby teeth, and a well-placed filling can last for years with good home care. The aim is to remove decay, seal the tooth, and restore its shape so chewing feels normal again. If decay is deep, the team will talk about a crown or a root canal before the work starts, so there are no surprises in the chair.

Invisalign

Invisalign uses a set of clear aligners to move teeth a little at a time. Each set is worn for about one to two weeks, then swapped for the next set in the plan. Because the aligners come out for meals and brushing, many adults and teens find them easier to live with than fixed braces. They suit people who want straighter teeth without metal brackets showing in photos or at work.

The plan starts with a scan or impressions, photos, and a talk about the end goal. Some people want front teeth lined up. Others want a bite that closes better or crowded lower teeth eased apart. The dentist will show how the teeth may move and how long the plan may take. Wearing the aligners for the hours asked each day is the part that makes the plan work. Missed hours slow progress, and lost aligners can add weeks.

After the last set, a retainer holds the result. Teeth have a habit of drifting back, so the retainer is not an extra. It is part of the treatment. Ask how often to wear it, how to clean it, and when to come back for a check. With steady wear, the new smile stays put.

Dental Implants

Dental Implants replace a missing tooth with a post placed in the jaw, a connector, and a crown shaped to match nearby teeth. The result can look and feel close to a natural tooth, and it lets you chew without a plate that moves. Implants can also hold a bridge or a denture steady, which helps people who have struggled with loose plates.

The process takes time, and that time has a purpose. After the post is placed, the bone needs weeks to settle around it. Then the crown is fitted and checked for bite and shade. Not every mouth is ready at once. Bone depth, gum health, smoking, and some health conditions all play a part. A careful exam and x-rays will show whether an implant is a good fit, whether bone care is needed first, or whether a bridge or partial denture would serve better. A Dental clinic Palm Beach Gardens patients can return to for each stage makes that slow process far easier to follow.

Care after an implant is much like care for a natural tooth: brush, floss around the post, and keep checkups. With that care, implants can last for many years. If the gum around an implant bleeds or feels sore, call early. Early care keeps a small problem small.

Emergency

Emergency dental pain rarely waits for a free afternoon. A tooth can break on a nut, a filling can come loose, or a dull ache can turn sharp overnight. When that happens, call the office as soon as you can and describe what happened, when it started, and what makes it worse. The team can often fit an urgent visit into the day and will tell you what to do until you are seen.

For a knocked-out tooth, time matters most. Hold it by the crown, not the root, rinse it gently without scrubbing, and try to place it back in the socket if you can do so without force. If that is not possible, keep it in milk or in your cheek and get to a dentist fast. For a broken tooth, save any piece you can find, rinse with warm water, and use a cold cloth on the cheek to ease swelling. For swelling with fever or trouble swallowing, seek urgent medical care at once.

After an emergency visit, ask what caused the problem and how to lower the chance of it happening again. A cracked tooth may point to grinding at night. A lost filling may point to decay under an old repair. Knowing the cause turns a one-off fix into a plan.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Cosmetic Dentistry is about how a smile looks and how it feels to use it. Veneers can cover chips, gaps, and stains that whitening cannot lift. Whitening can brighten teeth that have dulled with coffee, tea, or age. Bonding can fix a small chip in one visit. The best results start with a talk about what bothers you when you smile, not with a list of treatments.

Veneers are thin shells fitted to the front of teeth. They need a small amount of tooth shaping, and they are made to match the shape and shade you choose with the dentist. They are not right for every tooth. Teeth with heavy decay, gum disease, or heavy grinding may need other care first. A trial smile or a wax model can help you see the change before any final work is done.

Whitening works best on teeth that are healthy and free of large fillings on the front. The dentist will check for sensitivity, talk about the shade you can expect, and warn you that crowns and fillings do not whiten with the rest of the tooth. A steady plan, with care at home, keeps the result longer than a one-off rush.

Oral Surgery

Oral Surgery covers extractions, wisdom teeth, and other work inside the mouth that needs a surgical approach. A tooth may need to come out when it is badly broken, when decay has reached too far, or when a wisdom tooth presses on its neighbour. The dentist will explain why removal is the right call, what the visit will feel like, and how to heal well after.

After an extraction, rest matters. Bite gently on gauze as told, avoid straws and smoking for the first days, eat soft foods, and keep the area clean without hard rinsing. Pain that grows after the third day, a bad taste, or swelling that spreads should be reported at once. Most mouths heal well with simple care and a follow-up check.

Wisdom teeth deserve a careful look even when they do not hurt. An x-ray can show whether they have room to come through, whether they trap food, or whether they press on nearby teeth. Some people keep theirs for life. Others do better with them out before they cause pain. The choice should rest on what the x-ray shows and how the teeth sit, not on a guess.

How to choose the right care for you

Start with the problem that bothers you most, then ask what fixes it, what it costs, and what happens if you wait. A filling is often the right answer for early decay. A crown is often the right answer for a tooth that has lost a lot of structure. An implant is often the right answer for a missing tooth when the bone and gums are healthy. Invisalign is often the right answer when teeth are crowded or spaced and the bite is sound. Each answer should come with a reason you can repeat to a family member at home. At a Dental clinic Palm Beach Gardens visit, ask for that reason in plain words before you agree to treatment.

Bring a list of medicines, allergies, and past dental work to your visit. Tell the team if you feel nervous, if needles worry you, or if a past visit went badly. A good team will slow down, explain each step, and check in as they go. Nervous patients are common, and they deserve care that respects that fact. Short visits, clear talk, and a pause when you raise a hand can make a real difference. The team at Steven Weiss DDS will work at your pace and answer questions before any work begins.

Parents often ask when a child should first visit. A first check by age one, or when the first tooth appears, sets a calm pattern. Early visits are short, kind, and focused on teaching brushing and watching growth. Teens may need care for sports guards, straightening, or wisdom teeth. Ask about a guard if your child plays contact sport. A custom guard costs less than a broken tooth.

It varies by person. The post needs time to settle in bone, often several weeks or months, before the final crown is placed. Your dentist will give you a timeline after your exam.
